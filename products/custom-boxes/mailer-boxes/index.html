Compare internal and external box dimensions

Separate retail presentation from parcel protection.

A folding carton, rigid gift box and corrugated mailer perform different jobs in an order. Explain which components are inside the mailer and whether it travels on its own or inside another shipping box. Review the complete product and packaging combination rather than assuming a decorative finish provides transit protection.

Ask which board construction and closure are proposed for the actual packed weight and route. A board name or tidy sample photograph does not establish a transport-performance result. Agree any distribution checks before production approval.

Compare mailer boxes and shipping boxes

Plan print around the packing bench.

Shipping labels and tape can cover exterior artwork. Leave space for those practical elements, and identify the inside panels you want customers to see when opening the box. Mark important logo colors, barcodes and any printed instructions on the approved dieline.

Review the liner and artwork together. Check small text and color appearance on the selected surface, then inspect the folded sample around corners, locking tabs and folds. State any finishing request separately so it can be reviewed for the proposed construction.

Choose the right print proof

Send a brief your packing team can use.

Catalog mailer references start at 500 pieces. List quantities per size and printed design, including any different inserts. Ask how the mailer is supplied and assembled, what the sample needs to verify and which packing components are included in the quotation.

  • PHG reference or proposed mailer format, with the changes you need.
  • Complete contents, packed weight and internal dimensions with units.
  • Insert, wrapping, seal or tape requirements and the packing sequence.
  • Printed sides, artwork versions, important colors and label positions.
  • Quantity per design, destination, sample priorities and target arrival window.

Mailer box questions.

Check the packed order, the opening experience and the way your team assembles the box.

Should I send internal or external dimensions?

State which convention you are using and include the actual product measurements. Internal dimensions help define usable packing space; the finished outer parcel must also be checked for storage and shipping planning. Confirm both on the agreed drawing and packed sample.

Do all orders need the same insert?

List the smallest and largest product combinations and show how each is packed. Review whether one insert can hold those combinations or whether different layouts are needed. Do not approve only the most convenient sample arrangement.

Can I print the inside as well as the outside?

Mark each requested printed panel on your brief. Ask for the proposed print route, surface and cost for that layout, then review the assembled proof. Do not assume both sides are included because a reference image shows interior artwork.

Will a mailer be supplied ready to fill?

Confirm how the selected construction is delivered and which assembly, insert and sealing steps your team performs. Try the complete packing sequence with a sample before confirming a repeatable fulfilment workflow.
