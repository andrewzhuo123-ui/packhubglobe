Inside paper packaging production

Follow the making of your packaging.

Eight stages from preparation to assembly. The production route depends on your structure, material and finish.

  1. 01

    Plate making

    Prepare the plates and cutting tools around the approved artwork and dieline.

  2. 02

    Paper cutting

    Cut paper and board to the sheet sizes planned for the job.

  3. 03

    Offset printing

    Print the artwork with the agreed CMYK or spot-color specification.

  4. 04

    Coating

    Apply the selected varnish or coating to the printed surface.

  5. 05

    Lamination

    Apply matte or gloss film when the chosen specification calls for it.

  6. 06

    Corrugated mounting

    Mount the printed sheet to corrugated board for the selected box structure.

  7. 07

    Die cutting

    Cut and crease the printed sheets into the approved packaging shape.

  8. 08

    Folding & gluing

    Fold, glue and assemble the structure for its final packing checks.

Custom printed packaging, specified for your product.

A good packaging project connects product fit, printed branding and the way the finished order is handled.

Start with the complete contents

Share the product, dimensions or fill quantity, accessories and any existing retail pack. A carton around a bottle, a presentation box around a set and a mailer around the whole order create different requirements. Tell us how the package will be displayed, filled, carried or shipped so the structure can be reviewed around its real job.

Our catalog brings together packaging images and reference specifications across boxes, bags, pouches and printed accessories. Use a PHG reference to identify a direction, then explain the changes you need. This keeps the conversation tied to a specific example while leaving room to resolve dimensions, materials, closure and artwork for your brand.

Plan print and material together

Paperboard, kraft paper, rigid wraps, films and label materials interact differently with print and finishing. Identify your important logo colors and the surfaces customers will see. Discuss foil, embossing, coating or clear-film white ink where relevant to the selected reference rather than assuming the same instructions suit every material.

Review the right sample for each decision. A structural sample can answer fit and assembly questions, while a suitable printed reference helps with artwork and appearance. Keep approvals attached to the material, dieline and artwork revision that will actually be used.

Discuss your packaging project

Questions?
Start here.

Need help choosing a format? Tell us about your product.

Can I request several packaging products together?

Yes. Include the boxes, bags, cards and labels you are considering in one brief. List the quantity and artwork versions for each item so they can be reviewed separately.

What is the minimum order quantity?

Custom catalog products start at 500 pieces. List your quantity per size and printed design so the proposed production split can be included in your quotation.

Can I start without final dimensions or artwork?

Yes. Describe the product, approximate size or fill weight and intended use. Attach reference images or artwork directly in the enquiry form. Format, dimensions and artwork requirements can then be reviewed.

How are samples and production timing confirmed?

Identify what the sample needs to verify and share your target delivery date. The sample route and production schedule need to be confirmed for the selected specification; transit time is considered separately.

How do I discuss material or sustainability requirements?

State your destination market and intended use. Material, food-contact and environmental claims need documentation applicable to the actual structure. Paper-based or mono-material packaging should not be assumed recyclable in every market.
