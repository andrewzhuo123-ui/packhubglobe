How to choose a packaging supplier in China: checklist

Compare packaging suppliers by supplying entity, specification, sample scope, documentation, inspection and the full quotation basis.

By PackHubGlobe · Updated 5 November 2026

Worked exampleComparisonChecklistFAQ

Quick answer

Compare packaging suppliers using the same written specification, an identified supplying company and samples that answer your project’s risks. Ask who makes each component, what the quotation includes, which documents support important claims and how inspection and shipment responsibilities are agreed. A polished website, a low unit price or a certificate image alone is not enough to approve a supplier.

  • Keep an agreed specification with each comparable quotation.
  • Verify the scope of documents important to your order.
  • Record the approved sample and how later changes will be handled.

Identify the company and scope

Ask for the legal supplying name and clarify manufacturing, sourcing and subcontracting responsibilities. Match the quotation and commercial paperwork to the intended supplier. Where several companies or sites are involved, request a clear component-level responsibility map rather than assuming every product comes from one facility.

Compare evidence at the right level

Ask to review relevant production capabilities, sample construction and order-specific documents. If a certificate matters, check the issuing record and scope rather than relying on a cropped logo. If performance matters, request the construction, method, conditions and acceptance criteria behind the claim. Evidence for a different material or order may not answer your requirement.

Make the commercial comparison explicit

Keep dimensions, material, artwork, quantity, samples, packing and delivery basis consistent across quotes. Identify tooling, freight and inspection responsibilities separately. Agree change control, nonconformity handling and the reference that governs release. Obtain specialist advice where a transaction or destination requirement calls for it.

A worked procurement example

Illustrative buying scenario: a buyer receives three quotations for a gift-box range. The buyer discovers that one includes assembly, another assumes flat supply and another omits freight. The team requests revised quotes using the same specification, then reviews the proposed samples and the supplying entity. The revised comparison supports a decision without inventing supplier rankings, savings or customer endorsements.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating the cheapest unit price as the lowest complete order cost.
  • Assuming a certificate covers every product and process.
  • Leaving sample limitations and release responsibility unstated.

Use the comparison above to identify what is still unknown. Ask for a written answer or a suitable sample where a purchasing decision depends on that point. An unanswered requirement is easier to resolve before artwork, tooling or a production quantity is approved than after the order has been scheduled.

Your approval checklist

  • Keep an agreed specification with each comparable quotation.
  • Verify the scope of documents important to your order.
  • Record the approved sample and how later changes will be handled.

Keep this checklist with the specification and the people responsible for product, artwork and purchasing approval. Give each file and sample a clear revision. If an item remains open, identify who will answer it and which part of the order depends on the answer. This makes the approval useful to the next person reviewing the package, rather than leaving important decisions in an email thread or a photograph without context.

Turn the findings into a clear approval record

Record the reference reviewed, the conditions or contents used, the findings and the changes requested. Distinguish a design direction from an approved production specification. When several people are involved, identify who approves wording, structure, printed appearance and the commercial terms. Keep the final artwork and the relevant physical reference together. If a later revision changes one decision, review the affected parts explicitly rather than silently treating every earlier approval as valid for the new version.

Frequently asked questions

Must a supplier own every production step?

Assess whether the proposed responsibility, coordination and evidence fit your project. Ask explicitly which work is performed by which company rather than inferring ownership.

How should I review an FSC claim?

Use the current official certificate record and request the relevant order claim and documentation. A certificate image should not be treated as proof that every offered item is FSC-labelled.

Are samples enough to approve a bulk order?

Identify what each sample represents and agree production checks. A sample is useful evidence for specific decisions, not proof of every future item or delivery condition.
