Internal vs external box dimensions: how should you specify size?

Avoid quoting a box from an ambiguous measurement.

PackHubGlobe packaging notebook · General procurement guidance

Start with usable internal space

Internal dimensions describe the space intended for the contents. External dimensions describe the outside of the finished box and include the construction. The difference depends on board, structure and folds; there is no single allowance for every box.

Measure the complete product

Include caps, lids, protective bags and any other parts that remain inside the box. Specify orientation and the clearance needed for packing and removal. Give units with every measurement.

Allow for the insert and the closure

A fitted tray can occupy space that appears empty on a flat drawing. Closure flaps and opening access may also affect the usable layout. Record the agreed order of length, width and depth on the dieline.

Confirm the assembled sample

Measure and pack the sample, record its revision and confirm which dimensions are controlled in production. Outer shipping or shelf constraints should be specified in addition to product-fit requirements.

Bring the question into your project.

General guidance does not replace a specification or test for your actual product. Bring your format, contents, quantity, artwork and destination into the discussion. Prepare a packaging enquiry, compare sample types or explore the product formats.
