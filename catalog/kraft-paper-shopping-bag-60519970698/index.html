Size the bag around the purchase

Plan a paper bag around the complete retail purchase, including its product box, tissue and any additional items. Measure the height that remains below the handles and the depth needed at the base. A bag can appear large enough in a flat drawing yet be awkward to carry once filled. Consider the handover at the counter, a short walk and storage before use. Confirm the usable opening and the loaded shape with the intended retail purchase before approving the dimensions.

Paper, handles and reinforcement

Paper surface, handle style, attachment and base construction work together. Review the intended load and the points where the contents contact the bag. A narrow bottle, a sharp carton corner and a soft garment create different demands. Discuss reinforcement at the handle and base when relevant, and inspect the assembled sample with the intended contents. Do not derive a carrying limit from paper weight alone; the complete bag and the agreed use conditions need their own review.

Print coverage and coordinated branding

A paper bag often sits beside a box, card or label from the same range. Identify the most important shared brand color and compare samples together. Natural paper shade, coated surfaces and printed coverage can create different appearances from the same digital artwork. Review logos on both faces, gussets, handle positions and the base. Keep small text away from folds and handle openings, and confirm how foil or embossing will appear on the selected paper.

Check the filled bag

Inspect seam bonding, the base fold, handle comfort and the way the loaded bag stands. Test the intended lifting, handover and opening routine with the actual purchase. Check access below the handles, support at the base and contact with corners of the packed item. Record the sample construction and load used for evaluation. Changes to dimensions, paper, handles or adhesive can require a new review.
