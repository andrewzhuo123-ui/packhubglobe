Internal vs external box dimensions: how should you specify size?

Avoid quoting a box from an ambiguous measurement.

By PackHubGlobe packaging team · Updated 16 October 2026

Worked exampleComparisonChecklistFAQ

Start with usable internal space

Internal dimensions describe the space intended for the contents. External dimensions describe the outside of the finished box and include the construction. The difference depends on board, structure and folds; there is no single allowance for every box.

Measure the complete product

Include caps, lids, protective bags and any other parts that remain inside the box. Specify orientation and the clearance needed for packing and removal. Give units with every measurement.

Allow for the insert and the closure

A fitted tray can occupy space that appears empty on a flat drawing. Closure flaps and opening access may also affect the usable layout. Record the agreed order of length, width and depth on the dieline.

Confirm the assembled sample

Measure and pack the sample, record its revision and confirm which dimensions are controlled in production. Outer shipping or shelf constraints should be specified in addition to product-fit requirements.

Quick answer

Internal dimensions describe the intended usable space; external dimensions describe the outside of the finished package. Specify which measurement you mean, the unit and the orientation. Include both when product fit and an outer shelf, carton or postal constraint must be satisfied at the same time.

A worked procurement example

Illustrative brief: a buyer measures a bottle at 50 × 50 × 140 mm, including the cap, and requests a carton with an insert. Those are product measurements, not an approved box size. The supplier reviews the clearance, insert and closure, then returns an identified dieline. The buyer packs the assembled sample and records usable interior measurements and the final exterior limits before approving the structure.

Compare the decisions that matter

DecisionWhat to share or review
Product dimensionsMeasure every component that remains inside the pack, including protrusions and wrapping.
Internal dimensionsDefine the usable space and the agreed length, width and depth orientation.
External dimensionsRecord outer limits separately where shipping cartons, shelves or equipment impose constraints.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Omitting units or reversing length and width between emails.
  • Adding an assumed universal board allowance to every structure.
  • Ignoring the space occupied by an insert, folds or closure parts.

Use the comparison above to identify what is still unknown. Ask for a written answer or a suitable sample where a purchasing decision depends on that point. An unanswered requirement is easier to resolve before artwork, tooling or a production quantity is approved than after the order has been scheduled.

Your approval checklist

  • Actual product with cap and accessories
  • Labelled length/width/depth orientation
  • Internal or external convention
  • Units on every drawing
  • Insert, wrapping and packing clearance
  • Sample revision and controlled measurements

Keep this checklist with the specification and the people responsible for product, artwork and purchasing approval. Give each file and sample a clear revision. If an item remains open, identify who will answer it and which part of the order depends on the answer. This makes the approval useful to the next person reviewing the package, rather than leaving important decisions in an email thread or a photograph without context.

Turn the findings into a clear approval record

Record the reference reviewed, the conditions or contents used, the findings and the changes requested. Distinguish a design direction from an approved production specification. When several people are involved, identify who approves wording, structure, printed appearance and the commercial terms. Keep the final artwork and the relevant physical reference together. If a later revision changes one decision, review the affected parts explicitly rather than silently treating every earlier approval as valid for the new version.

Frequently asked questions

Can I calculate exterior size by adding twice the board thickness?

Do not use that as a universal rule. Folds, wraps, joints and construction can affect the relationship; confirm it on the actual structure.

Which size should be on my quote request?

Label what you know. Product dimensions can start a fit review; a confirmed internal box size can support a more defined request. Add any external limit separately.

Should I measure a flat box?

A blank can support manufacturing checks, while the assembled pack answers fit and use questions. Agree the measurement method for the dimension being controlled.
