What do you need for a custom packaging quote?

Prepare a brief that lets suppliers quote the same specification.

By PackHubGlobe packaging team · Updated 16 October 2026

Worked exampleComparisonChecklistFAQ

Start with the product and quantity

Describe the product, packed weight or fill weight, approximate dimensions, and quantity per size and design. A total quantity without the number of artwork versions can hide important setup differences.

Define the package and artwork

Identify the box, bag, pouch, card or label format. Share approximate dimensions with units, print coverage, number of printed sides and finish preferences. If the format is undecided, describe the packing and delivery problem instead.

Clarify sampling and timing

State what the sample needs to verify: structure, fit, printed color, sealing or a specific finish. Include your launch window and delivery country. Production timing should be confirmed against the final specification and approvals; shipping is a separate part of the plan.

Compare quotations on the same basis

Ask suppliers to separate tooling or setup, samples, production, packing and freight where applicable. Confirm the quoted specification, quantity per design, delivery basis, payment conditions and quotation validity in writing.

Quick answer

A useful packaging quote brief identifies the product, package format, dimensions, quantity per design, material direction, artwork and delivery country. Start with the information you know, label open decisions and ask suppliers to quote on the same basis. PackHubGlobe catalog products start at 500 pieces; splitting an order across sizes or artwork versions should be discussed explicitly.

A worked procurement example

Illustrative brief: a skincare brand needs 2,000 cartons in two artwork versions, plus a shared insert card. The buyer lists 1,000 cartons per version and a separate quantity for the card, describes the bottle with its cap attached, and identifies the delivery country. This makes the component quantities visible. It does not assume that all 2,000 cartons share one setup or that the card is automatically included in the carton price.

Compare the decisions that matter

DecisionWhat to share or review
DimensionsState internal box size or the product dimensions to be fitted; add units and orientation.
Artwork versionsList the quantity for each printed design, colorway and size rather than only a grand total.
Commercial basisAsk which items are included: samples, tooling, production, export packing and freight.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Comparing quotations that use different materials or delivery terms.
  • Treating an image as a complete production specification.
  • Leaving artwork splits and delivery country out of the first enquiry.

Use the comparison above to identify what is still unknown. Ask for a written answer or a suitable sample where a purchasing decision depends on that point. An unanswered requirement is easier to resolve before artwork, tooling or a production quantity is approved than after the order has been scheduled.

Your approval checklist

  • Product and complete component list
  • Quantity per size and printed design
  • Dimensions with units and measurement convention
  • Material and finishing priorities
  • Artwork status and sample decisions
  • Destination and target arrival window

Keep this checklist with the specification and the people responsible for product, artwork and purchasing approval. Give each file and sample a clear revision. If an item remains open, identify who will answer it and which part of the order depends on the answer. This makes the approval useful to the next person reviewing the package, rather than leaving important decisions in an email thread or a photograph without context.

Turn the findings into a clear approval record

Record the reference reviewed, the conditions or contents used, the findings and the changes requested. Distinguish a design direction from an approved production specification. When several people are involved, identify who approves wording, structure, printed appearance and the commercial terms. Keep the final artwork and the relevant physical reference together. If a later revision changes one decision, review the affected parts explicitly rather than silently treating every earlier approval as valid for the new version.

Frequently asked questions

Can I request a quote without a dieline?

Yes. Send product measurements or a sample and explain how the package will be used. A structural direction can be reviewed before final artwork is placed.

Does a 500-piece MOQ cover several designs?

List the quantity for each design. The catalog starting quantity does not mean different sizes and artworks can be combined automatically; ask for the proposed order split in the quotation.

What should I compare besides unit price?

Compare the same specification, quantity, sample scope, setup charges, packing and shipping basis. A lower unit price can describe a different package or exclude necessary work.
