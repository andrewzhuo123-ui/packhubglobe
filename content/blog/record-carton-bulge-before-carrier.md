# How do I record a carton bulge before handing it to a carrier?

I can see a carton bulge at dispatch and lose the evidence as soon as someone presses down the lid or opens the package.

**I photograph the carton as found, record the widest external spans without flattening it, and identify the affected face and closure. I compare the result with the packing criteria and carrier requirements before I release or repack it.**

I preserve the shape first. Then I take measurements and decide whether the parcel can continue to dispatch.

## What photographs should I take before moving the carton?

A close-up can show a bowed wall and leave me unable to tell which side of the parcel it came from.

**I photograph the full parcel, the affected face from the side, and the nearby seam or closure. I include a scale reference where it can be shown without changing the shape.**

### I connect the wide view to the close-up

I put a short identifier beside the parcel and take a view that shows its orientation on the packing bench. I label the faces in the record using stable landmarks such as the hinge or closing seam. Then I photograph the affected face from an angle that shows the curve. I avoid pressing a ruler into the board. I place it nearby in the same plane when I can. If the image cannot show the offset reliably, I include a separate reading in the record.

I photograph the tape or lock where it meets the affected panel. I look for a lifted edge, a gap at a corner or a change in the flap overlap. I also show a neighboring face, because a panel under pressure may distort the seam on another side. I use the [warehouse damage inspection guide](/blog/3pl-damage-inspection-record/) to keep the photographs in a sequence another person can follow.

I note when the photos were taken and whether the carton had been sitting in a stack. If the deformation changes after I move the parcel, I keep both sets of photos and describe the change. I keep order references in the inspection record and use a masked label when I share an image outside the team.

## How do I measure the carton without flattening the bulge?

A tape pulled around a bowed wall measures its surface. A straight span tells me how much space the parcel occupies.

**I use two straight references to capture the outermost points on each axis. I record the unrounded spans and keep the measured dimensions separate from any carrier rounding.**

### I record both the outer span and the affected position

I set the carton on a level surface in its dispatch orientation. I bring straight references near opposite sides without compressing the board. I measure the distance between the references. I repeat for the other two axes and note which measurement includes the bulge. If the widest point is near one corner, I mark that location on the photograph. I use the [guide to inside and outside dimensions](/guides/internal-vs-external-box-dimensions/) to distinguish these measurements from the nominal carton size.

UPS's shipping dimension guidance discusses measuring irregular packages using their extreme points.[^1] I check the requirements for my service and retain the raw measurements. I record any rounding separately so the next person can reconstruct the rating inputs. FedEx also explains why parcel dimensions can affect rated weight.[^2] I use the closed parcel measurements if I need to compare the original package with a repacked one. I keep both measurements in the same record. When a second person measures the parcel, I ask them to use the same orientation and note any difference before choosing the dispatch dimensions.

## What tells me whether I should hold the parcel?

A bulge can be stable on the bench and still affect the closure, label surface or product. I check the agreed limits before dispatch.

**I hold the parcel if the closure is strained, the package falls outside an agreed limit, or I cannot verify the product is adequately protected. I compare it with an accepted packed sample.**

### I separate a dimension issue from a protection issue

I check the carton against the current packing instruction. If the face is bowed but the label and closure remain in the required condition, I still check the actual dimensions and service limits. If a seam is separating or a product can be seen through a gap, I set the parcel aside. I ask the team to inspect it before sending it. I do not use a carrier's willingness to accept a parcel as the only evidence that the product is protected.

I compare it with an accepted pack using the same carton and load. I check whether the bulge appears in all samples or only in one. A single parcel may contain a misplaced insert or an extra item. A repeated bow can point to the fit of the carton, the board, or the standard packing sequence. I use the [mailer and shipping box guide](/guides/mailer-boxes-vs-shipping-boxes/) when the team needs to review the outer construction.

I document the decision and its owner. If I hold a group of cartons, I record which orders or carton batches may be affected. I keep one example in the condition found where possible so the next person can review the same evidence. If the issue affects a scheduled collection, I tell the dispatch team what is ready and what remains on hold.

## How do I record what changed after repacking?

A corrected carton can hide the reason for the original bulge. I keep the before and after records together.

**I photograph the contents as I open the held carton, identify the cause, and record the revised packing method. I repeat the measurement and closure checks before releasing the new parcel.**

### I look for evidence inside the package

I open the carton in its normal orientation and photograph the contents before lifting them out. I check whether the tallest item sat under the raised area or whether a pad shifted toward the side wall. I look for pressure marks inside the lid and on the product. If the carton had been stacked, I also check whether the deformation could have come from the way it was stored. I describe what I observed and distinguish it from the cause I suspect.

I repack using an approved alternative when one exists. If I need a different carton or insert, I verify that the product still fits and is restrained. I check the closure again and record the new external spans. I confirm which dimensions and label belong to the final parcel. I keep the before and after photographs with the same order reference.

If the problem repeats, I give the buyer or converter the affected carton revision and the load details. I ask for a correction that addresses the measured problem and test it on representative packs. I update the packing instruction only after the new method meets the agreed checks. The dispatch team can then compare future parcels with a clear reference.

## Conclusion

I record the bulge before changing the parcel, measure its true outer spans, and verify the corrected pack before release.

[^2]: [FedEx dimensional weight guidance](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/packaging/what-is-dimensional-weight.html) explains how parcel dimensions can affect rated weight.
