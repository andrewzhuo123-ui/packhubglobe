# How do I set up an approval hold before my supplier buys material?

I can ask a supplier to quote a board grade and find it has already placed an order while my sample review is still open.

**I put the material purchase hold on the order and ask the supplier to acknowledge it. I identify the material, allowed preparation, release conditions and person who can approve a commitment. Any early purchase gets a separate written limit.**

I use the hold at the point where the supplier would commit money or reserve stock on terms that are hard to reverse. I keep sample and production decisions visible beside it.

## What should I put on hold?

A broad instruction to stop work can delay useful preparation. A vague instruction can leave the supplier free to buy more than I intended.

**I name the material, grade and quantity covered by the hold. I state which activities can continue, such as checking availability or requesting a sample from existing stock.**

### I separate preparation from financial commitment

I ask the supplier which steps it can take without spending money on my behalf. It may be able to check mill stock, request a lead-time estimate or prepare a drawing. I ask whether a reservation carries a cancellation charge or obliges it to buy the stock later. If it does, I treat that as a commitment for this purpose. I record what the supplier may do while the hold is open and confirm whether the rule also covers subcontractors.

| Activity | What I ask the supplier to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| Check availability | Whether the check carries any commitment |
| Request sample stock | Which quantity and costs are authorized |
| Reserve material | When a reservation becomes chargeable |
| Place a material order | Whether written release is required |

I list the grades and quantities the hold covers. If the supplier can make samples from standard stock, I state whether it may use that stock within a defined limit. If a custom coating or color needs a full minimum, I list that purchase separately. I use the [packaging quote checklist](/guides/packaging-quote-checklist/) to keep sample, material and tooling costs visible. I ask for confirmation that the supplier's buyer received the same instruction as its salesperson.

## What evidence should release the hold?

A supplier can receive approval for a print proof and treat it as permission to buy the board. I say which decision each approval covers.

**I identify the specification, sample or other evidence required before purchase. I name the approver and ask the supplier to wait for an explicit release tied to the correct revision.**

### I identify which approval controls the material choice

I list the checks that can change the material. If I am comparing two board grades for stiffness or finish, I wait for the responsible team to accept one. If the structure works and only a small copy correction remains, I ask whether the material decision can be made separately. I use the [packaging sample types guide](/guides/packaging-sample-types/) to identify the sample that supports the decision. I keep the approved sample reference with the material grade and drawing revision.

Esko describes packaging approval workflows for reviewing artwork and related files.[^1] I use a separate release for the purchase step even when the team records its artwork approval in the same system. I identify who can approve the material, which quantity the approval covers and what the supplier may buy. If the approval applies to one product only, I put that limit in the message.

I ask the supplier to acknowledge the release. Oracle's purchasing documentation describes supplier acknowledgment of purchasing documents.[^2] I use that idea to check that both sides have the same record, whether the exchange takes place through a portal or another agreed channel. If a message contains the wrong revision or amount, I correct it before the supplier commits.

## How do I handle a long lead time without releasing the full buy?

A board mill can require an early decision while my sample review still needs a week. I compare what the early purchase actually saves.

**I ask for the latest order date and the smallest useful commitment. If I authorize an early buy, I state the material, quantity, cost exposure and conditions in writing.**

### I put a price on the early decision

I ask the supplier to show how the early purchase affects delivery. I check whether the material is the only step driving the schedule. If a finishing slot is also unavailable, buying stock today may do little for the dispatch date. I compare the date with the hold in place and the date under the proposed exception. I confirm what the supplier can reserve without a fee and when that option expires.

I ask what happens if the sample fails. I want to know whether the stock can be returned, used on another order, or stored until I have a new plan. I identify the costs the buyer would bear under the agreed terms. If I need only enough for a trial, I ask whether the converter can buy that amount or use a standard sheet. I avoid approving the full order merely because a smaller trial needs material.

I also check whether buying earlier narrows the design choices. If I authorize a specific finish or thickness, I confirm that later artwork or structural changes remain workable. I write down the limit and any expiry date on the exception. I keep the rest of the hold in place until the required checks are complete.

## How do I keep the hold clear after the specification changes?

A supplier may keep working from a release that applied to an earlier grade. I check who has the revised instruction and what has already been ordered.

**I identify the affected material and replace the earlier instruction with an updated written record. I ask the supplier to confirm purchases already made and hold any new commitment until the revised release is accepted.**

### I track status by material and revision

I keep a short record showing the grade, quote, current approval status and permitted quantity. If the structure changes, I check which purchases it affects. If the supplier already placed an authorized order, I ask which part of it can be canceled or redirected. I keep the resulting decision with the order instead of leaving it in a separate message chain. The [print proof approval record guide](/blog/packaging-print-proof-approval-record/) gives me a way to keep sample and version references visible.

I tell the buyer, designer and supplier which release is current. If a second person requests a change, I ask them to route it through the named approver. I check that the supplier can identify the authorized material in its own purchasing record. I also confirm what the warehouse should expect if both the earlier and later grades are on site.

After receipt, I compare the delivered material with the release and accepted sample. I record any early commitment and whether it improved the schedule. If the release covered only a trial quantity, I check that the unused balance was never added to a later invoice without a separate approval. When I set up the next order, I can use that evidence to decide whether the same hold or an earlier decision point makes sense.

## Conclusion

I define the material commitment, name the release authority, and keep exceptions tied to a specific grade, quantity and approved cost.
