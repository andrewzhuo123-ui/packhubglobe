Begin with the product and filling route

Pouch selection begins with the contents, fill quantity, storage conditions and filling method. Whole beans, powders and irregular snacks occupy different volumes at the same weight. Describe any sensitivity to oxygen, moisture, light or aroma as separate requirements. The stand-up shape is only one part of the specification. A filled trial helps establish headspace, zipper clearance and the seal area before artwork and a material structure are approved.

Material and functional features

Review the proposed layer structure and thickness with the intended product. A zipper, valve, window, spout or hang hole changes how the pouch is used and manufactured, so include these features in the brief from the beginning. A metallic appearance does not establish a complete barrier specification. Request relevant material documentation and agree the checks needed for the actual application. Product shelf life and direct-contact suitability require evidence for the selected structure and intended conditions.

Artwork around seals and gussets

Map the artwork onto the agreed flat dimensions, gussets and sealing areas. Identify the front panel, back information panel and any surface that becomes less visible after filling. Confirm white-ink coverage for clear or metallic materials and keep variable information readable. Important copy should remain clear of the agreed seal and trimming tolerances. Review the printed result together with the filled shape, because a flat proof cannot show every fold or the final shelf presentation.

Filled-pack validation

Use the actual product or an agreed representative fill to evaluate capacity, standing behavior and closure access. Review seal conditions with the filling process rather than assuming a stock sample proves the final pack. Check for product trapped in the seal and confirm how the customer opens and recloses the pouch. Keep the approved material, dimensions and feature set together in the specification. Any change in filling conditions, contents or laminate needs an appropriate review before repeat production.

Customize the package as a complete system

Bring the contents, package and printed information into one brief. Tell us which reference features you want to retain and which you want to change: dimensions, material, closure, insert, color, printed panels or finishing. Supply a logo and describe the artwork status, even if the design is still being developed. Structural decisions should be resolved before important text is positioned on a final dieline. If several components need to coordinate, list their individual quantities and explain how they fit together at packing and delivery.
