Choose the carton around the product.

A folding carton is a retail pack, not automatically a protective shipping box. Begin with the complete contents, how the carton will be filled and the way the customer opens it.

Specify board and print together.

Start from the material reference on a selected carton, then confirm board type, thickness and the surfaces to be printed. Do not substitute paper weight for board thickness or assume two boards with the same weight will fold identically.

Identify the panels carrying important logo colors. Discuss CMYK or spot colors, coating, lamination and any foil or embossing as separate choices. Check finishing near folds, glue areas and small type on the proposed board.

Measure the contents before the box.

For a bottle or jar, include the cap, pump, label and any insert in the fit review. Send maximum product dimensions and explain whether the quoted carton size is internal or external. Agree the order of length, width and height on the drawing.

A structural sample should answer fit, insertion, closure and removal questions. If an insert is needed, review it with the carton and the product together. Assess the outer shipping pack separately when the carton will travel through parcel delivery.

Prepare a folding carton quotation.

Catalog references start at 500 pieces. State the quantity for each size and artwork version rather than combining different cartons into one total. The written quotation confirms the production split, tooling, sample route and freight basis.

  • Product dimensions, weight, orientation and any accessories or insert.
  • Carton structure or PHG reference, with the changes you need.
  • Board reference, printed panels, colors and finishing priorities.
  • Quantity per size and design, artwork status and required sample checks.
  • Delivery country, target arrival window and the proposed shipping pack.

Folding carton questions.

Resolve the structure before treating the artwork as production-ready.

Can the carton protect a glass bottle during shipping?

Review the bottle, carton, insert and outer shipping pack as one system. A folding carton on its own does not establish transit protection. Share the packed weight and delivery conditions so the sample and transport checks can be agreed.

Do I need a final dieline before sending artwork?

You can send an early design for discussion. Place production artwork on the approved dieline after dimensions, folds, glue zones and opening orientation are confirmed. Keep the artwork revision attached to the approved structure.

Will a plain sample confirm the final printed appearance?

A plain structural sample helps check fit and assembly. It does not approve final color, foil, coating or print registration. Identify which printed proof or reference is needed for each appearance decision.

How are flat delivery and assembly agreed?

Ask how the selected carton will be supplied, which gluing is completed and which steps your packing team performs. Try the proposed assembly sequence with a sample before confirming the order.
