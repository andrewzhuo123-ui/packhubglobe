Paper bag load testing: what should a buyer review?

Check the complete bag with the product it will carry.

By PackHubGlobe packaging team · Updated 16 October 2026

Worked exampleComparisonChecklistFAQ

Define the intended load

Record the contents, weight, shape and carrying time. A smooth product and an item with sharp corners create different stresses. Paper weight alone does not establish the performance of a finished bag.

Include handles and the base

Handle attachment, reinforcement, seams and base folds work together. Load the assembled bag and review how the contents sit, whether handles feel comfortable and where stress concentrates.

Match the conditions

Dry indoor carrying, humidity and repeated handling can produce different results. Agree test conditions and acceptance criteria that reflect the intended use instead of relying on an undocumented lifting demonstration.

Keep a reference

Identify the tested construction, sample and load. If paper, adhesive, handles or dimensions change, review whether the original result still applies. A sample test should not become an unlimited carrying guarantee.

Quick answer

Review a paper bag as an assembled package carrying the intended contents. Specify the load, product shape, carrying routine and relevant conditions. Paper weight alone does not establish a finished-bag carrying limit; handles, attachment, seams, base and reinforcement all belong in the evaluation.

A worked procurement example

Illustrative evaluation: a retailer uses the same bag size for a soft garment and a boxed glass item. The buyer checks both filled arrangements because their shapes stress the bag differently. The review records handle comfort, product position, base behavior and the agreed carrying routine. The findings apply to the documented constructions and conditions; they are not turned into an unlimited weight guarantee for every product or environment.

Compare the decisions that matter

DecisionWhat to share or review
ContentsRecord product weight, corners, contact points and the way items sit in the base.
ConstructionIdentify paper, handle attachment, reinforcement, seams and base folds.
ConditionsAgree carrying, repeated handling, storage and exposure relevant to the intended use.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using paper GSM as the only performance requirement.
  • Testing an empty bag or a different product arrangement.
  • Repeating a result after changing handles, adhesive or dimensions without review.

Use the comparison above to identify what is still unknown. Ask for a written answer or a suitable sample where a purchasing decision depends on that point. An unanswered requirement is easier to resolve before artwork, tooling or a production quantity is approved than after the order has been scheduled.

Your approval checklist

  • Complete load and product arrangement
  • Handle and base construction
  • Paper and adhesive references
  • Intended carrying routine
  • Agreed conditions and acceptance criteria
  • Sample identity and findings

Keep this checklist with the specification and the people responsible for product, artwork and purchasing approval. Give each file and sample a clear revision. If an item remains open, identify who will answer it and which part of the order depends on the answer. This makes the approval useful to the next person reviewing the package, rather than leaving important decisions in an email thread or a photograph without context.

Turn the findings into a clear approval record

Record the reference reviewed, the conditions or contents used, the findings and the changes requested. Distinguish a design direction from an approved production specification. When several people are involved, identify who approves wording, structure, printed appearance and the commercial terms. Keep the final artwork and the relevant physical reference together. If a later revision changes one decision, review the affected parts explicitly rather than silently treating every earlier approval as valid for the new version.

Frequently asked questions

Can you quote a guaranteed load from a photograph?

A photograph does not establish the construction or test conditions. Share the intended load and use so a suitable sample evaluation can be planned.

Does a larger bag always carry more?

No general assumption should be made. Dimensions change the load path, base and handling, so review the complete construction.

When should the bag be checked again?

Review the evidence when relevant materials, handles, adhesive, size, load or use conditions change. Keep the new construction identifiable.
