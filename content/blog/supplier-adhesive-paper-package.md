# How do I ask my supplier about an adhesive in a paper package?

I can approve a carton from its board specification and still have no record of what holds the window or seam in place.

**I ask the supplier to identify the adhesive, the surfaces it joins, and where and how it is applied. I request evidence for the intended use and agree on what the supplier must report before making a change.**

I start with the joints on a finished pack. That gives the converter a clear list of questions and gives my team something it can check at receipt.

## Which joints should I include in the request?

A single carton may contain several adhesive applications. I need to know which joint a supplier's answer describes.

**I map the seams, window patches, liners, labels and other bonded parts on the finished pack. I give each relevant joint a reference and record the surfaces it joins.**

### I follow the parts through converting and packing

I take an approved sample and open it in the order a customer would. I identify the glued seam, the position of any window patch and the bond beneath a liner. I check whether an adhesive strip is applied by the converter or by my warehouse. I photograph each area and mark it on a separate reference drawing. If I cannot see a bond without damaging the box, I ask the converter to show its position on the drawing or supply an unassembled example.

| Joint | What I want to identify |
| --- | --- |
| Side seam | Board faces and length of the bonded area |
| Window patch | Film, board and attachment pattern |
| Label | Label stock, adhesive and receiving surface |
| Closure strip | Applied part and intended closing sequence |

I use the [dieline preparation guide](/guides/prepare-packaging-dielines/) to keep structural and review notes clear. I check which parts must hold throughout shipping and which are meant to separate during use. A window intended to stay fixed needs a different acceptance check from a removable label. I also ask if the same adhesive is used across the listed joints. I keep separate rows even when one adhesive serves several locations because the surfaces and purpose can differ.

## What information do I need from the supplier?

A description such as hot melt tells me the type of adhesive. It may leave me unable to identify the actual approved joint on a repeat order.

**I request the product or controlled supplier code, technical information and the proposed application. I ask which specifications are guaranteed and which conditions the converter must verify.**

### I connect the adhesive reference to the two surfaces

I identify the board face, coating and any film or label stock that will be bonded. I ask for the adhesive reference that applies to that exact combination. If the supplier cannot share a commercial code, I ask whether its controlled reference can identify the approved construction and trigger notice of a change. I ask what written evidence can be provided for any requirement the quality team sets. I use the [packaging quote checklist](/guides/packaging-quote-checklist/) to make the documentation part of the buying scope.

Henkel's packaging adhesive guidance describes options for different applications.[^1] I use the product information for the selected adhesive and ask the converter to explain how it applies it. I ask whether the bond is a strip, spot or continuous line and where it sits. I record any effect on assembly or the time needed before the carton can be handled. If the supplier changes the application pattern during a trial, I ask it to document which version produced the sample.

I also ask which facility performs the work and whether another converter supplies a bonded subassembly. I want the supplier responsible for the finished box to identify how each joint is controlled. I keep the answer with the drawing revision.

## How do I verify that the bond works in the finished pack?

A strong bond on flat pieces can behave differently at a fold or after the pack is loaded and held. I check the assembled joint.

**I inspect the bond after forming and after representative storage and handling. I verify whether it holds or releases as intended, then retain the approved sample and test conditions.**

### I test the joint where it has to work

I assemble the sample using the production sequence. I check whether the glued flap remains flat, whether a window lifts near a corner, and whether any adhesive squeezes into a visible area. I load the product and note whether it presses on the bonded part. I inspect the same joint after the interval when the warehouse usually handles or packs it. I include representative temperatures or other conditions when those can affect the use. I use the [packaging sample types guide](/guides/packaging-sample-types/) to decide when the converter needs to make a production-representative sample.

I ask the quality team what failure means for that joint. A structural seam may need to remain closed, while a removable label may need to peel without damaging the surface. I document the observation and look at both sides when a joint separates. I record whether paper fibers came away, the surface lifted, or the adhesive appeared to release. Those are clues to review with the converter. I avoid treating one observation as a complete diagnosis.

If the sample differs from production, I write down what it can demonstrate and what still needs a check. I confirm which result is required before the supplier releases the bulk order.

## How do I handle material claims and later substitutions?

An adhesive change can be invisible at receipt. It can still affect opening, performance or the evidence behind a disposal statement.

**I tie the adhesive and application to the approved construction. I require notice of changes and review the affected performance, contact or disposal requirements before approving a substitution.**

### I separate the bond decision from the material assessment

I record the version and any relevant supplier evidence alongside the sample. If the pack carries a disposal instruction, I ask which information the reviewer needs for the complete construction. The 4evergreen design guideline discusses adhesive considerations in fibre-based packaging.[^2] I use the relevant assessment and market guidance for the actual package. A successful bond check tells me that the joint worked under the conditions I tested. I keep evidence for disposal or recycling as a separate record.

At reorder I ask whether the adhesive, coating or application changed. If a substitute is proposed, I ask for its reference and compare the joint in the same location. I check how the change affects the opening or packing sequence. If the same part uses a different surface now, I repeat the relevant bond check. I document which requirement the new sample met and which order the approval covers.

I ask the supplier how it can connect the accepted construction to delivered lots. I give receiving the sample and any visible acceptance points it can check. If a joint fails, those records help me ask about the exact production version instead of requesting a general statement about the adhesive.

## Conclusion

I identify each bond, verify the approved adhesive on the real pack, and reassess the joint and material evidence whenever the construction changes.

[^2]: [4evergreen Circularity by Design guideline](https://4evergreenforum.eu/about/circularity-by-design-guideline/) covers adhesives and other design considerations in paper-based packaging.
