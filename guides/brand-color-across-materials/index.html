Brand color across packaging materials: how do you coordinate it?

Plan one graphic identity across cartons, pouches, bags and cards.

PackHubGlobe packaging notebook · General procurement guidance

One brand, several substrates

Uncoated paper, coated board, clear film and metallic surfaces reflect light differently. A single artwork color value cannot describe every visual effect across these materials.

Choose a hierarchy of references

Identify the most important brand color, acceptable relationships between components and the viewing context. Share physical references where useful and confirm the print route for each product.

Account for the structure

Folds, seals, embossing and coatings change the way a design is seen. Place logos and slogans where they remain readable after forming, filling and handling rather than only on a flat layout.

Approve a coordinated set

Review representative samples together under agreed conditions. Record each material and artwork version. Coordination means managing the relationships within the range, not claiming every printed surface will be physically identical.

Bring the question into your project.

General guidance does not replace a specification or test for your actual product. Bring your format, contents, quantity, artwork and destination into the discussion. Prepare a packaging enquiry, compare sample types or explore the product formats.
