Define the job of the printed piece

A card, hang tag or booklet should make one next step easier: understanding the product, reading care instructions, identifying a variant or presenting a gift. Decide whether the buyer reads it before purchase, during unboxing or later in use. That decision guides finished size, paper, fold and copy hierarchy. Avoid squeezing every message onto a small face; a clear front and a more detailed reverse can be easier to use than one crowded design.

Paper, attachment and finishing

Choose the paper surface and thickness against handling, print and attachment. For a hang tag, specify the hole position, string and how the tag hangs on the product. For an insert, check fit inside the complete package. For folded pieces, review panel order and how the reader opens them. Foil, embossing and die cutting can add emphasis, but fine details need a suitable artwork and sample review. Keep practical information readable after the piece is attached or folded.

Artwork and information hierarchy

Prepare final wording before arranging the layout. Make product names, instructions and important care information easy to locate. Review text at actual size, including the smallest line and any QR code. Keep cut, fold, hole and finishing layers clearly identified in the artwork. If the printed piece is part of a coordinated packaging range, compare its paper and print reference alongside the box, bag or label rather than approving each in isolation.

Approve the assembled context

Place the sample in its actual setting: inside a pack, attached to a product or on a counter. Check that the important printed face is visible and that handling is practical. Where folding or attachment is specified, review its position, sequence and effect on readable information. Confirm wording, translations and codes with the product owner before production. Keep the approved file and material reference together so repeat orders do not accidentally mix old copy with a new layout.
