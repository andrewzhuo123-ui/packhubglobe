Packaging materials: paperboard, greyboard or corrugated?

Understand paperboard, greyboard and corrugated construction, then compare product fit, printing surfaces and sample requirements.

By PackHubGlobe · Updated 4 November 2026

Worked exampleComparisonChecklistFAQ
Quick answer

Paperboard is a starting point for cut-and-fold retail cartons; greyboard commonly provides the core of wrapped rigid boxes; corrugated board combines liners and fluting for a different structural direction. Choose the complete construction around the product, packing method and distribution route. Material names alone do not specify thickness, strength, recycled content or suitability for contact with a product.

Separate the core from the visible surface

A rigid gift box can have a greyboard core with a separate decorative wrap. A printed corrugated pack can combine a print-facing sheet with a fluted construction. When comparing quotes, list the layers and finishes rather than assuming the exterior image identifies the complete material.

Compare dimensions and assembly

Board thickness and fold behavior affect usable space, creases and closures. Measure the product in its final orientation, including protective components. Request a sample of the proposed construction and check how it is packed, stacked and removed. Specify outer-size limits as well as internal fit when shelf or freight constraints matter.

Ask for relevant evidence

If a project needs a sourcing, contact or performance claim, identify the exact construction and intended use first. Ask which documents and tests address that requirement. An attractive kraft appearance does not establish recycled content, and a paper specification does not establish finished-package performance.

A worked procurement example

Illustrative buying scenario: a buyer needs a retail carton and an outer shipping pack for the same glass jar. Paperboard is reviewed for the printed retail unit; corrugated construction and cushioning are reviewed for the shipping configuration. A separate greyboard gift-box option is costed for a presentation set. The buyer compares the complete assembled packages rather than choosing one material name for every layer.

Compare the decisions that matter

DecisionWhat to share or review
Paperboard cartonReview board grade, thickness, creasing, printing and product retention.
Greyboard rigid boxReview core, wrap, corners, insert and assembled shipment volume.
Corrugated packReview liners, flute construction, closure, cushioning and the distribution test plan.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Comparing only paper weight while ignoring construction.
  • Calling a kraft-colored surface proof of recycled content.
  • Using a material sample as a finished-package test result.

Use the comparison above to identify what is still unknown. Ask for a written answer or a suitable sample where a purchasing decision depends on that point. An unanswered requirement is easier to resolve before artwork, tooling or a production quantity is approved than after the order has been scheduled.

Your approval checklist

  • List core, outer surface, inner surface and coatings.
  • Record thickness and size conventions in the specification.
  • Match requested documentation to the agreed material and order.

Keep this checklist with the specification and the people responsible for product, artwork and purchasing approval. Give each file and sample a clear revision. If an item remains open, identify who will answer it and which part of the order depends on the answer. This makes the approval useful to the next person reviewing the package, rather than leaving important decisions in an email thread or a photograph without context.

Turn the findings into a clear approval record

Record the reference reviewed, the conditions or contents used, the findings and the changes requested. Distinguish a design direction from an approved production specification. When several people are involved, identify who approves wording, structure, printed appearance and the commercial terms. Keep the final artwork and the relevant physical reference together. If a later revision changes one decision, review the affected parts explicitly rather than silently treating every earlier approval as valid for the new version.

Frequently asked questions

Is heavier paper always better?

Weight is one input. Structure, fiber, thickness, folds, adhesives and intended load also matter. Compare the assembled package in use.

Can corrugated board be used for presentation?

It can be considered where its structure and surface suit the design. Review the opening, printing and insert rather than assuming presentation requires a rigid box.

Does kraft mean recyclable?

Appearance alone does not establish recyclability. Review the complete construction and the collection system relevant to the intended market.

What should I send for material selection?

Send product dimensions and weight, contact conditions, required appearance, quantity, packing method and delivery route. Include any documentation requirement.
