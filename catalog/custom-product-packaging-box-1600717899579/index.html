Retail fit and product information

A folding carton brings structure and product information into a small space. Start with the complete product, including its cap, sleeve, label and any protective bag. Decide the display orientation and the panel that will face the shopper. Reserve room for the product name, variant, instructions and codes before filling the dieline with decoration. The carton should be easy to assemble, close and reopen during normal handling without forcing the contents against a flap or seam.

Board, tuck and base selection

Choose paperboard together with the carton structure. Straight-tuck, reverse-tuck, self-lock and other base directions create different assembly and opening routines. Review the load at the base, the number of packing steps and the way the product is removed. Board weight is only part of the specification: surface, thickness, grain, scores and glue areas also need review. Ask for a structural sample sized around the real contents before treating a nominal dimension as production-ready.

Print and finish planning

Place the main brand panel, side information and secondary copy on the current dieline. Separate foil, embossing and selected coating layers from the color artwork. Leave the agreed clear areas around folds, glue flaps and codes; the prepress team should confirm the required tolerances for the chosen route. Dark solids and pale colors can behave differently across paper surfaces. Approve print and finishing using a reference suitable for the actual material rather than a screen image alone.

Assembly and shelf review

Use the sample to check closure engagement, panel alignment and how a row of cartons looks together. Test the packing sequence with the people or equipment that will fill the cartons. If a bottle or jar can shift, review the dimensions or an internal support instead of simply making the board heavier. Check readability after the carton is formed. Record revisions to both the dieline and artwork so a corrected label panel is not placed on an obsolete structural file.

Customize the package as a complete system

Bring the contents, package and printed information into one brief. Tell us which reference features you want to retain and which you want to change: dimensions, material, closure, insert, color, printed panels or finishing. Supply a logo and describe the artwork status, even if the design is still being developed. Structural decisions should be resolved before important text is positioned on a final dieline. If several components need to coordinate, list their individual quantities and explain how they fit together at packing and delivery.

Plan the sample and quality checks

Choose the sample according to the decisions still open. A structural sample helps review size, assembly and product access. A printed reference helps review artwork and selected appearance questions; confirm how closely its material and print route represent the order. Agree the important cosmetic and functional checks before production. Inspect the package with its intended contents and record the approved revision. A change to the product, material, closure or insert may require the relevant decision to be reviewed again rather than carried over automatically.

MOQ, quotation and delivery planning

The minimum order quantity for this catalog reference is 500 pieces. State the quantity for each size and printed design so the quotation can identify the proposed production split. Ask for tooling or setup, sampling, product supply, export packing and freight to be explained where applicable. Production scheduling follows the agreed specification and approvals; delivery also depends on the destination and shipping arrangement. Include your required arrival window in the first message, and keep any change to artwork, quantity or packing visible when comparing a revised quotation.
