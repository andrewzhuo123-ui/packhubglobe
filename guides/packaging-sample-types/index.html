Packaging sample types: what does each one verify?

Choose samples by decision rather than by appearance.

By PackHubGlobe packaging team · Updated 16 October 2026

Worked exampleComparisonChecklistFAQ

Stock samples

Stock samples can help you understand a material direction, general format or approximate fit. Their usefulness depends on how close they are to the intended structure. They do not establish the final artwork or complete specification.

Structural samples

Plain or unprinted structures help check dimensions, assembly, product fit and opening. Use the actual product when possible and document the changes to the dieline.

Printed samples

A printed sample helps inspect layout and certain print decisions. Confirm whether its material and print route represent the planned order before treating it as a color or finishing standard.

Production-representative samples

When risk calls for a sample close to the planned construction, agree what it represents and what remains to be tested. Record filling, sealing, contact or transit checks separately instead of assuming every sample proves them.

Quick answer

Choose a sample by the question you need it to answer. A stock sample helps screen a format; a structural sample checks fit and assembly; printed and production-representative samples address different appearance and manufacturing questions. State what remains unverified rather than calling every sample a final approval.

A worked procurement example

Illustrative plan: a brand first reviews a stock drawer box to understand the opening direction. A custom structural sample follows with the actual jewelry and insert layout. A printed reference then addresses the selected wrap and logo treatment. The buyer keeps a short approval record for each stage. This sequence separates useful early decisions from the checks that still depend on the final construction and artwork.

Compare the decisions that matter

DecisionWhat to share or review
Stock sampleUseful for screening a general direction; may not match final dimensions or print.
Structural sampleUseful for size, assembly and product access; does not automatically establish color.
Production referenceAgree the construction and processes it represents, plus any remaining validation.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using one sample to approve decisions it does not represent.
  • Testing a package without the actual accessories or final fill.
  • Failing to keep a revision record when several samples circulate.

Use the comparison above to identify what is still unknown. Ask for a written answer or a suitable sample where a purchasing decision depends on that point. An unanswered requirement is easier to resolve before artwork, tooling or a production quantity is approved than after the order has been scheduled.

Your approval checklist

  • Question the sample must answer
  • Contents used in the evaluation
  • Material and construction represented
  • Artwork and dieline versions
  • Checks, findings and requested changes
  • Reference retained for production

Keep this checklist with the specification and the people responsible for product, artwork and purchasing approval. Give each file and sample a clear revision. If an item remains open, identify who will answer it and which part of the order depends on the answer. This makes the approval useful to the next person reviewing the package, rather than leaving important decisions in an email thread or a photograph without context.

Turn the findings into a clear approval record

Record the reference reviewed, the conditions or contents used, the findings and the changes requested. Distinguish a design direction from an approved production specification. When several people are involved, identify who approves wording, structure, printed appearance and the commercial terms. Keep the final artwork and the relevant physical reference together. If a later revision changes one decision, review the affected parts explicitly rather than silently treating every earlier approval as valid for the new version.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start with a stock sample?

Yes, when it helps answer an early question about format or material. Record where it differs from the intended order.

Does a printed sample prove transit protection?

A visual sample and a distribution evaluation answer different questions. Agree the relevant loaded-pack checks separately.

What happens after a sample change?

Identify the changed structure, material or artwork and decide which approvals need to be repeated. Do not mix references from different revisions.
