Roll label unwind direction: what should you confirm?

Prevent a correctly printed label from arriving on an unusable roll.

By PackHubGlobe packaging team · Updated 16 October 2026

Worked exampleComparisonChecklistFAQ

Start with the applicator

Obtain the labelling-machine specification or the hand-application workflow. The label face, leading edge and direction it leaves the roll must match the equipment and container orientation.

Describe the roll

Specify label width and pitch, gap, liner, core and outer diameter. Supplier numbering conventions can differ, so include a diagram showing exactly how the label feeds rather than relying on a number alone.

Review print orientation

A portrait label can be printed correctly yet arrive rotated relative to the application path. Mark the top of the artwork, the leading edge and the adhesive side on the approval reference.

Run an application trial

Check dispensing, detection, alignment and adhesion on the actual product. Confirm any changes before the complete quantity is produced. Keep the approved unwind drawing with the artwork revision.

Quick answer

Confirm label orientation using an unwind drawing that shows the roll, printed face, leading edge and direction of feed. Include core size, outer diameter, label dimensions and the applicator requirements. Supplier numbering systems can differ, so an unambiguous diagram is more useful than an unexplained direction number.

A worked procurement example

Illustrative order: a bottle brand has a portrait label and a machine that requires one particular edge to leave the roll first. The buyer supplies the applicator specification and marks the top of the artwork on an unwind drawing. A small application trial checks dispensing and orientation before the full order. The label is also read and scanned on the curved container, where its appearance differs from the flat approval image.

Compare the decisions that matter

DecisionWhat to share or review
Feed directionShow which edge reaches the applicator first and how the printed face is oriented.
Roll geometryConfirm core, maximum outside diameter, web width, pitch and gap.
ApplicationCheck dispensing, alignment, detection and adhesion on the real container.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Approving a direction number without a drawing.
  • Specifying the label size but omitting the liner and roll requirements.
  • Checking a code only on the backing sheet instead of the labelled product.

Use the comparison above to identify what is still unknown. Ask for a written answer or a suitable sample where a purchasing decision depends on that point. An unanswered requirement is easier to resolve before artwork, tooling or a production quantity is approved than after the order has been scheduled.

Your approval checklist

  • Applicator or hand-application workflow
  • Label face and leading edge diagram
  • Core and outside diameter
  • Width, pitch, gap and liner
  • Container surface and application conditions
  • Trial result and approved roll drawing

Keep this checklist with the specification and the people responsible for product, artwork and purchasing approval. Give each file and sample a clear revision. If an item remains open, identify who will answer it and which part of the order depends on the answer. This makes the approval useful to the next person reviewing the package, rather than leaving important decisions in an email thread or a photograph without context.

Turn the findings into a clear approval record

Record the reference reviewed, the conditions or contents used, the findings and the changes requested. Distinguish a design direction from an approved production specification. When several people are involved, identify who approves wording, structure, printed appearance and the commercial terms. Keep the final artwork and the relevant physical reference together. If a later revision changes one decision, review the affected parts explicitly rather than silently treating every earlier approval as valid for the new version.

Frequently asked questions

Can a correct label print still be unusable?

Yes. Incorrect roll geometry or orientation can prevent the label from feeding as intended. Review print and supply format together.

Are unwind numbers universal?

Do not rely on that assumption. Confirm the meaning with a drawing and the equipment specification.

Should labels be tested on the container?

Yes. Container shape, surface and application conditions affect alignment, appearance and adhesion. Use representative finished products for the trial.
