Apparel packaging: choose bags, mailers, boxes and tags

Coordinate apparel bags, ecommerce mailers, gift boxes and hang tags using folded garment dimensions, packing workflow and artwork approvals.

By PackHubGlobe · Updated 4 November 2026

Worked exampleComparisonChecklistFAQ
Quick answer

Choose apparel packaging around the folded garment, sales channel and packing workflow. Retail paper bags carry a purchase, mailers contain a shipment, gift boxes organize presentation and tags communicate product information. Specify each component separately, then check the complete set together. A coordinated graphic identity does not mean every component should use the same material or dimensions.

Begin with the packed garment

Measure the garment as staff will actually fold it, including protective bags, tissue or accessories. Compare the smallest and largest variants. Leave working room for packing and removal without creating unnecessary movement. A photograph of an unfolded garment is not sufficient to approve the finished package size.

Define each component’s job

List the retail bag, garment bag, mailer, box, tag, card and seal only where the purchase journey needs them. State which components travel together. Include handle comfort for carry bags, closure for mailers and attachment for tags. This prevents a visually coordinated set from failing at the packing station.

Coordinate appearance across surfaces

Share artwork versions and physical color references. Paper, coated board, film and card can produce different appearances from the same artwork values. Review a representative set together and record which reference governs each material. Final text, sizes, care information and codes should be approved by the product owner.

A worked procurement example

Illustrative buying scenario: an apparel buyer prepares a paper bag and hang tag for retail, plus a protective garment bag and outer mailer for online orders. A separate gift-box option is assessed for a seasonal set. Staff pack the folded garments and accessories into each proposed combination. The buyer compares fit and handling before approving the coordinated artwork, rather than ordering every component from a mood board alone.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using garment size labels as package dimensions.
  • Leaving tag attachment out of the specification.
  • Expecting matching color values to look identical on film and paper.

Use the comparison above to identify what is still unknown. Ask for a written answer or a suitable sample where a purchasing decision depends on that point. An unanswered requirement is easier to resolve before artwork, tooling or a production quantity is approved than after the order has been scheduled.

Your approval checklist

  • Trial the actual folded garments and accessories.
  • List quantities per component, size and artwork.
  • Approve the complete graphic set with clear material references.

Keep this checklist with the specification and the people responsible for product, artwork and purchasing approval. Give each file and sample a clear revision. If an item remains open, identify who will answer it and which part of the order depends on the answer. This makes the approval useful to the next person reviewing the package, rather than leaving important decisions in an email thread or a photograph without context.

Turn the findings into a clear approval record

Record the reference reviewed, the conditions or contents used, the findings and the changes requested. Distinguish a design direction from an approved production specification. When several people are involved, identify who approves wording, structure, printed appearance and the commercial terms. Keep the final artwork and the relevant physical reference together. If a later revision changes one decision, review the affected parts explicitly rather than silently treating every earlier approval as valid for the new version.

Frequently asked questions

Can one size cover every garment?

Compare the packed dimensions of the full range. A common size can simplify the order but may leave too much movement or make larger items difficult to pack.

Do paper bags replace shipping mailers?

Their jobs differ. A retail bag must be assessed separately from the closed parcel and its distribution requirements.

Should tags and bags use the same print colors?

They can share a brand direction, but material and print differences need review. Compare physical references together rather than relying only on screen values.

What should I send for a coordinated quote?

Send folded garment dimensions, a component list, quantities per size and artwork, delivery market and any reference images or existing brand standards.
