# How do I specify a coffee pouch beyond the valve and zipper?

I can buy a coffee bag with a valve and zipper and still miss the main protection needs. I need a complete coffee pouch specification.

**I specify a coffee pouch through product type, fill weight, filling process, material structure and closure system. I review the valve as one component and validate the sealed pack with the actual coffee. I support any shelf-life or disposal claim with evidence for that construction and use.**

I start with the coffee and the packing process. I then review the bag shape, the material and the checks that must happen before a printed order.

## What should I tell the supplier about the coffee?

I can give only a fill weight and receive a bag that is awkward to fill. I need to describe the product and process as well.

**I state whether I pack whole beans or ground coffee, the target weight, filling method and expected storage route. I describe the planned roast-to-pack workflow and ask the supplier to review suitable valve and material options. I do not set a shelf-life claim from bag size alone.**

### I describe the actual filling job

I record the target weight and the product form. I ask for a filled sample because different coffee products can occupy different volumes at the same weight. I include the working space needed above the product for the zipper and final seal. I do not assume a catalog capacity label predicts the fit of my coffee.

I describe whether staff fill by hand or use equipment. I note the available sealing method and ask the supplier what settings and trials the proposed material needs. I do not copy a sealing temperature from a different film. I want a documented trial on the selected construction with the filling team's equipment.

### I include the distribution plan

| My brief item | Why I include it | What I ask to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product form | I distinguish beans and grounds | I check fill and valve suitability |
| Target weight | I define the selling unit | I test volume and seal clearance |
| Filling process | I identify equipment constraints | I run a sealing trial |
| Storage and sales route | I define the exposure | I plan validation evidence |

I use the [stand-up and flat-bottom comparison](/guides/stand-up-vs-flat-bottom-pouches/) when I need to decide on shape. I keep that shape choice separate from barrier and food-contact review. A bag that stands well on a shelf can still require a different material structure for the product and its intended storage period.

## What do the valve and zipper each do?

I can treat a valve, zipper and final seal as one protection feature. I want to know which job each component is meant to perform.

**I review the valve for the product's gas-release needs, the zipper for repeated opening and the final seal for the unopened pack. I confirm compatibility and placement with the pouch supplier. I do not treat the zipper as a substitute for the agreed final sealing process.**

### I review the valve as a component

Wipf describes coffee degassing valves that allow gas to leave while limiting outside entry through the valve.[^1] I use that basic function to ask the supplier for a valve suited to my product and material. I do not assume every valve has the same construction or that a valve proves the whole pouch is airtight.

I ask for the valve specification, attachment method and proposed position. I discuss whether ground coffee needs a particular valve arrangement. I also check that the valve will not be covered by a label or pressed awkwardly by the packing layout. I keep the sample reference so the production order does not quietly use a different component.

### I check opening and final closure

I fill, seal and open a sample as a customer would. I examine the tear path and whether it leaves enough access to the zipper. I close the zipper several times with the intended contents present. I do not infer a leak performance level from the fact that the zipper feels firm.

I ask the filling team to keep the sealing area clean and follow the agreed trial method. I identify where product dust or a trapped bean could interfere with the final seal. I record visible checks and any test methods separately. A neat seal appearance can be useful, but I need suitable evidence when a specific seal or package performance claim matters.

## How should I review barrier and food-contact evidence?

I can choose a kraft look and assume it protects the coffee for a fixed period. I want evidence that matches the whole laminate and use.

**I request the complete material structure, relevant barrier data and food-contact documentation for the intended market and conditions. I check the test conditions and scope. I validate the filled product before I print a shelf-life claim or assume a material change is equivalent.**

### I ask what the numbers describe

I ask whether oxygen and moisture transmission data describe the flat film, a component or the finished pack. I record the units and test conditions. I do not compare two numbers when temperature, humidity or method differ. I also ask how valves, seals and handling are considered in the complete packaging assessment.

I describe the storage and distribution environment that my product will face. I do not turn a laboratory film result into a guaranteed number of months for coffee quality. I plan product validation with people qualified to assess the coffee and packaging system. I keep the acceptance criteria and sampling schedule in that plan.

### I match food-contact scope

FDA explains that food-contact substances are assessed for their intended use and applicable authorization.[^2] I therefore ask for documents that fit the chosen structure, food type and use conditions. I do not accept the phrase FDA approved as a complete explanation of every component or every application.

I keep food-contact, shelf-life and recyclability claims separate. A document that addresses one does not automatically establish the others. I ask for new review when the supplier changes a film layer, adhesive, ink system or valve. I also compare any paper-looking outer layer with the full structure, because a kraft surface does not tell me the barrier or disposal route of the inner layers.

## What should I approve before ordering printed bags?

I can finish the artwork before the filling trial and then need to change the bag. I want the technical decisions to guide the final print layout.

**I approve filled fit, sealing, component placement and the artwork on the selected pouch construction. I keep the accepted specification and sample version together. I confirm quantity per design and validate changes before I repeat the order.**

### I combine the physical and artwork checks

I use the supplier's current dieline after the pouch size and features are agreed. I keep text away from seals, gussets, zipper areas and the valve position as specified by the supplier. I check the panel in its filled shape, because a flat PDF can hide a fold that changes readability. I also reserve space for variable batch or date information.

I review artwork claims alongside the supporting evidence. I do not place a recycling symbol on the bag simply because the main film uses one polymer family. I check the full construction and destination route. I also keep any proposed storage or shelf-life statement in the product's approval process rather than letting the graphic designer infer it.

### I save a reorder specification

I record dimensions and their convention, material layers, thickness, print, valve, zipper and seal requirements. I include the accepted sample and artwork references. I list the quantity per size and design. I also confirm how the empty bags will be packed and stored before filling.

I use [flexible pouch options](/products/flexible-pouches/) to describe the starting format and the [rollstock versus premade workflow](/blog/rollstock-or-premade-pouches-filling/) if my filling process may change. I request a revised sample when a process or material change could affect the result. I want the next order to repeat a documented system, rather than a bag that only looks similar to the first one.

## Conclusion

I specify the whole coffee pouch system. I approve fill, seal, barrier evidence and artwork together before I commit to printed stock.

[^2]: [FDA: food packaging and food-contact substances](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-ingredients-packaging/food-packaging-other-substances-come-contact-food-information-consumers) — intended use is central to food-contact review.
