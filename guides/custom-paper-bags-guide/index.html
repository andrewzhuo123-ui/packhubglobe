Custom paper bags: materials, handles and print finishes

Specify custom paper bags by contents, dimensions, paper, handle attachment, reinforcement, artwork and carrying checks with the intended load.

By PackHubGlobe · Updated 4 November 2026

Worked exampleComparisonChecklistFAQ
Quick answer

Specify a custom paper bag as a complete carrying system: paper, dimensions, side and base construction, handle attachment, reinforcement and print finish. Begin with the actual contents and carrying conditions. Choose appearance and handles alongside a loaded sample review; paper weight alone does not establish bag strength. Confirm the construction and required checks in the written specification.

Measure the load and the opening

List every item that travels in the bag, including tissue, an inner box or protective wrap. Include the space needed to place and remove the contents. Check whether handles obstruct the opening and how the bag sits when loaded. Give width, gusset and height conventions explicitly to avoid comparing differently measured bags.

Review paper and handles together

Kraft and coated-paper directions create different visual and print choices. Twisted paper, flat paper, ribbon or rope-style handles also change comfort and attachment details. Review the proposed combinations rather than choosing a handle photograph separately from the bag structure. Ask which reinforcement is included at the top and base.

Check the finished bag in use

Use representative contents and agreed carrying conditions. Look for stress at handle attachments, seams, folds and contact with sharp product corners. If humidity or repeated handling matters, include it in the review plan. Keep results tied to the tested construction; do not turn one demonstration into an unlimited load guarantee.

A worked procurement example

Illustrative buying scenario: a gift buyer needs a bag for a boxed candle and a small card. The buyer compares a kraft-paper direction and a coated-paper direction with suitable handle proposals. Both are reviewed with the same complete contents, including the box corners and packing tissue. The selected construction and artwork are then recorded for the quote. No invented lifting result is necessary to explain the decision.

Paper and finishCompare appearance, print coverage, coating and the complete material construction.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing bag height without allowing for handle attachment.
  • Approving paper weight as a complete carrying specification.
  • Using an empty-bag photograph as evidence of load performance.

Use the comparison above to identify what is still unknown. Ask for a written answer or a suitable sample where a purchasing decision depends on that point. An unanswered requirement is easier to resolve before artwork, tooling or a production quantity is approved than after the order has been scheduled.

Your approval checklist

  • Pack all intended contents in an assembled sample.
  • Record paper, handles, reinforcement and finish together.
  • Agree load review conditions and how construction changes are assessed.

Keep this checklist with the specification and the people responsible for product, artwork and purchasing approval. Give each file and sample a clear revision. If an item remains open, identify who will answer it and which part of the order depends on the answer. This makes the approval useful to the next person reviewing the package, rather than leaving important decisions in an email thread or a photograph without context.

Turn the findings into a clear approval record

Record the reference reviewed, the conditions or contents used, the findings and the changes requested. Distinguish a design direction from an approved production specification. When several people are involved, identify who approves wording, structure, printed appearance and the commercial terms. Keep the final artwork and the relevant physical reference together. If a later revision changes one decision, review the affected parts explicitly rather than silently treating every earlier approval as valid for the new version.

Frequently asked questions

What dimensions should I send?

Send the contents and required usable space, then identify width, gusset and height with units. Clarify whether measurements are internal or finished external values.

Which handle is strongest?

Compare the complete bag and attachment under the intended load. Handle material alone does not establish a finished bag’s carrying performance.

Can I add foil or embossing?

Request a review of the chosen paper, artwork and finish position. Approve a suitable physical reference and confirm the final process in the quote.

Does a kraft bag prove an environmental claim?

No. Ask for evidence relevant to the complete construction and the specific claim. Color and surface appearance are not sourcing or recyclability documentation.
