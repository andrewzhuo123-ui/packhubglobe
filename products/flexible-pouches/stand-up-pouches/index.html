Start with the contents and filling method.

A bottom-gusset pouch needs a fit and filling check with your actual product. A fill weight from another product is a reference, not a capacity guarantee for your powder, tea, snacks or pet treats.

Review the complete material structure.

Use the selected reference to start the material conversation, then identify what the contents need from the complete pouch. Share sensitivity to moisture, oxygen, light, aroma or puncture, together with storage conditions and intended use.

A material name or a photograph does not establish barrier performance, food-contact suitability or shelf life. Request documentation and testing appropriate to the actual structure and destination market. Confirm any window, valve or other feature for the selected order rather than assuming it is included.

Match the pouch to your packing equipment.

Tell us whether filling is manual or by machine, what the product is like during filling and how the top seal is made. Share the opening and sealing requirements from your equipment supplier where available.

Use a filling trial to review pouch handling, contamination of the sealing area, final seal and closure access. A resealable zipper supports repeated opening; it should not be treated as a substitute for the agreed initial seal.

Stand-up pouch questions.

Approve the filled pack as well as the flat pouch.

Can I choose a size from the advertised fill weight?

Use it only as a starting point. Different products occupy different volumes at the same weight. Confirm the size with your contents, intended headspace and sealing clearance before approving the pouch.

Does a zipper make the pouch ready for retail filling?

Confirm the complete filling and sealing sequence. The opening must suit the filling tool, and the product should leave the final seal area clean. Review the zipper and first-opening arrangement on a filled sample.

Does a foil-looking pouch guarantee a particular shelf life?

No. Appearance does not identify the full film structure or measured barrier. Shelf-life decisions require evidence for the actual product, package, sealing process and storage conditions.

Can several designs share one 500-piece order?

List the quantity for each size and artwork version. Ask for the proposed print route, setup costs and minimum per version in the quotation; do not assume different designs can be combined without changing the commercial terms.
