# Do I need a structural sample or a printed packaging sample?

I can pay for a beautiful sample that answers the wrong question. I decide what I need to verify before I choose the sample route.

**I use a structural sample to review size, fit, assembly and closure. I use an appropriate printed sample to review artwork, material appearance and specified finishes. I may need both, and I keep transport or product-performance testing separate from visual approval.**

I choose samples through four decisions: the risk, the construction, the print scope and the final approval record. I want each sample to earn its place in the process.

## Which question should my first sample answer?

I can ask for a sample before I know whether fit or print is the main risk. I give the sample a clear purpose first.

**I list the unresolved decisions and start with the one that could force the largest change. I usually resolve uncertain dimensions and packing geometry before final decorative print. I ask the supplier to state which features the proposed sample includes and excludes.**

### I rank the open decisions

I write down what I do not yet know. I may need to check whether a lid clears a bottle cap, whether a pouch fits the fill volume or whether a handle is comfortable under the intended load. I do not combine those questions into a vague request for a premium sample. Each needs an observable check.

I consider what happens if the check fails. A wrong box size can force a new insert and artwork layout. A wrong slogan can be corrected in the file without changing the structure. I therefore resolve the geometry early when it remains uncertain. I still keep the brand direction in view so the eventual printed sample represents a coherent design.

### I define the sample scope

| My open question | My starting evidence | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the product fit? | I inspect a structural sample | I cannot infer final color |
| Is the text positioned well? | I review artwork on the pack | I cannot infer transit protection |
| Does the finish look right? | I inspect an agreed physical reference | I cannot infer every production unit |
| Will the pack survive the route? | I use a suitable test plan | I cannot replace it with a photo |

I use the [packaging sample types guide](/guides/packaging-sample-types/) to discuss terminology with the supplier. I ask the supplier to confirm its own sample process because labels such as development sample or pre-production sample can describe different levels of fidelity. I want the actual construction and purpose in writing.

## How do I assess a structural sample?

I can place the product inside once and call the fit approved. I want to test the loading, closure and removal that happen in real use.

**I assess the structural sample with the actual product and normal accessories. I check loading, movement, closure, removal and assembly. I record material differences from production and identify which observations need confirmation on the final construction.**

### I reproduce the packing arrangement

I bring the real product, not just a drawing, when possible. I include its retail carton, protective wrap and accessories. I follow the planned packing sequence and note awkward steps. I ask a second person to repeat the process so I can see whether the sample relies on careful handling that will not be practical at dispatch.

I inspect the closure with the product loaded. I look for pressure, bowing and contact with vulnerable parts. I open the pack and check whether the customer can remove the item without pulling against a label, cap or sharp edge. I do not solve every tight area by adding loose space. I ask the designer to distinguish necessary working clearance from uncontrolled movement.

### I record construction limits

I ask whether the sample uses the intended board, film, thickness and assembly method. A hand-built sample may reveal useful geometry while differing from production at folds, edges or glued areas. I mark those differences in the review record. I do not assume that a sample made from another material establishes production performance.

I keep load and transport checks separate from fit approval. ISTA describes different test procedures for packaged products and distribution systems.[^1] I discuss the relevant route with a qualified provider when that evidence is needed. I do not turn a successful fit check into a claim that the pack is certified or safe through every delivery route.

## What should I expect from a printed sample?

I can assume every printed sample uses the final production process. I ask how it was made before I judge its color and finish.

**I confirm the sample's print method, material, finish and construction before review. I identify which elements represent production and which are approximations. I compare artwork and physical appearance against agreed references, and I request additional evidence for features the sample cannot show.**

### I establish the reference

I match the sample to the current artwork and technical drawing. I inspect text, panel orientation, image placement and the opening sequence. I compare the sample with the approved PDF so I can distinguish a file error from a physical construction issue. I do not let an attractive finish distract from a wrong variant name or missing instruction.

I ask the printer how color should be judged on this sample. I check whether a digital print is being used to approximate a different production process. I also ask whether foil, embossing or a coating is real or simulated. I can use an approximation for layout review, but I keep the final appearance approval open when the sample does not represent that feature.

### I inspect the finished surfaces

Adobe's print guidance explains how marks, bleed and output settings relate to printed artwork.[^2] I use the supplier's specific drawing and settings rather than impose a generic allowance. I examine edges, folds and areas where the design crosses panels. I check small type at final size and inspect any technical lines that should not appear in the final print.

I handle the sample in the way the customer will. I observe rubbing, opening and surface marks, while keeping informal handling separate from a defined performance test. I take photos under consistent conditions for the project record. I also keep the physical reference when a photo cannot capture the paper feel or finish that the team is approving.

## How do I turn sample approval into a repeatable order?

I can approve a sample in conversation and lose the details before production. I need a record that connects every decision to the purchased specification.

**I save the sample identifier, specification, artwork revision and approval limits in one order record. I list corrections and review the revised evidence before approval. I require another review when changes affect fit, material, print or the agreed performance checks.**

### I make the acceptance record specific

I label the sample and record its date. I attach the current drawing and list the components included. I identify which features are accepted and which still need correction. I avoid a broad message such as looks good when the sample only represents structure and the final print remains unreviewed.

I mark corrections by location and expected result. I ask the supplier to show a revised sample or other suitable evidence for the issue. I then review the whole revised item, because a change to one part can affect another. A wider insert slot may improve loading and also reduce retention. I want both effects visible before I close the approval.

### I preserve the production reference

I keep the final specification with dimensions, materials, print details, quantity per design and packing instructions. I identify the accepted samples in that record. I ask the supplier to acknowledge the same references and explain any production difference. I do not assume that a familiar product photo defines the full order.

I use the [print proof approval checklist](/blog/packaging-print-proof-approval-checklist/) for the artwork stage and the [sampling service brief](/services/sampling/) to request the right physical evidence. I also review the first production delivery against the agreed reference and inspection plan. I save any approved revision so a later reorder starts from the accepted specification rather than an outdated sample on a shelf.

## Conclusion

I choose samples by the question they answer. I keep fit, print and performance approvals specific, then connect them to the final order record.
