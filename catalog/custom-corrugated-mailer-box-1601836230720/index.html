Design around the packed parcel

A mailer is a complete parcel system, not only an outer box. Measure the product together with its retail pack, insert, wrapping and any accessories. Describe the way the order travels: packing bench, courier handling, returns and final opening. Too much clearance can allow movement; too little can make packing slow or force pressure onto the contents. Review a loaded structure before adding decorative finishing, especially for glass, coated products and items with projecting corners.

Board and closure decisions

Specify the corrugated construction, finished internal dimensions and closure together. A flute description alone does not establish the performance of the finished parcel. Locking tabs, glue areas, tape or a tear strip influence how the box is packed and opened. For a return journey, discuss whether the customer needs a second closure or another shipping carton. The selected board and closure should be evaluated with the actual packed weight and expected handling conditions.

Printing for shipping and unboxing

Compare the outside shipping surface with the inside presentation surface. Shipping labels, tape and courier marks may cover exterior artwork, while the inside lid offers a cleaner brand moment. Leave practical space for delivery labels and avoid placing essential messages across folds, tears or closure points. On a natural kraft surface, review the artwork against the selected board shade. A screen preview cannot show every interaction between ink, liner texture and the finished folded structure.

Parcel sample review

Pack the sample using the planned insert, closure and working method. Check whether the product moves, whether tabs remain seated and whether opening damages printed information. Photograph the complete arrangement before and after the agreed checks. Transport testing should match the distribution route and the product/package combination; a tidy sample photograph is not a transit-performance result. Keep the approved packing method with the dieline so later changes in filler or product size are not overlooked.

Customize the package as a complete system

Bring the contents, package and printed information into one brief. Tell us which reference features you want to retain and which you want to change: dimensions, material, closure, insert, color, printed panels or finishing. Supply a logo and describe the artwork status, even if the design is still being developed. Structural decisions should be resolved before important text is positioned on a final dieline. If several components need to coordinate, list their individual quantities and explain how they fit together at packing and delivery.

Plan the sample and quality checks

Choose the sample according to the decisions still open. A structural sample helps review size, assembly and product access. A printed reference helps review artwork and selected appearance questions; confirm how closely its material and print route represent the order. Agree the important cosmetic and functional checks before production. Inspect the package with its intended contents and record the approved revision. A change to the product, material, closure or insert may require the relevant decision to be reviewed again rather than carried over automatically.

MOQ, quotation and delivery planning

The minimum order quantity for this catalog reference is 500 pieces. State the quantity for each size and printed design so the quotation can identify the proposed production split. Ask for tooling or setup, sampling, product supply, export packing and freight to be explained where applicable. Production scheduling follows the agreed specification and approvals; delivery also depends on the destination and shipping arrangement. Include your required arrival window in the first message, and keep any change to artwork, quantity or packing visible when comparing a revised quotation.
