Choose the bag for its actual job

Garment protection, retail carrying and parcel shipping are different jobs. State whether the bag will be displayed, stored, opened repeatedly or used as an outer shipping layer. Include the folded product size and any items placed alongside it. The usable interior depends on the closure and seal areas as well as the flat bag dimensions. Review clarity, opacity and the packing experience with a physical sample before selecting a printed design.

Film and closure specification

Define the material direction, thickness convention, finished size and closure in one specification. Zippers, adhesive flaps, hang holes and ventilation features should be reviewed where relevant to the product and distribution. A film name alone does not establish puncture, seal or carrying performance. Describe the actual contents and handling conditions so the sample check can target the right risks. Keep material and environmental claims tied to the structure and destination rather than to a general appearance.

Printed branding on flexible surfaces

Flexible film changes shape when it is folded, filled and handled. Position the logo and important text where they remain readable on the packed bag. Clear and translucent films may require a white-ink decision; opaque surfaces need their own color reference. Keep artwork away from seal areas and the working parts of the closure. Review a filled sample for readability, opacity and scuffing against the actual garment or product instead of approving only a flat visual.

Packing and opening trials

Try the intended packing sequence using the real contents. Check whether the product slides in cleanly, whether the closure has enough working space and whether any sharp edge stresses the film. For a resealable direction, inspect opening and reclosing under the expected use. For a mailer, review the full parcel arrangement and label position. Record the material, size and closure used in the trial so a later design change does not silently alter the approved packaging.
